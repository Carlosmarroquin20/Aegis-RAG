# 0026 — Route simple questions to a small model, by a rule-based classifier

**Status:** Accepted

## Context

Every answer is generated by the one configured model. On the hosted provider that is a
large model (`openai/gpt-oss-120b`), and most of what it is asked does not need one: the
retrieval evaluation's 141 questions are almost all single lookups ("Do you deliver on
Sundays?"), which a small model answers from the retrieved passage just as well, in less
time and at a fraction of the price per token. Some questions do need the large model:
comparisons, explanations, calculations, several questions in one, summaries.

A router is a component that decides, per question, which model answers. The questions that
decide its design are how it tells the two kinds apart, what happens when it is wrong, and
what happens when the small model is not there.

The two mistakes have different costs. A simple question sent to the large model costs money,
which is what happens today on every question. A complex question sent to the small model
costs a worse answer. ADR 0009 already measured how much worse: with the hosted model gone,
the local 3B model contradicted the documents in two of three answers.

## Decision

**A `RoutingLLMClient` behind the same `LLMClientPort`.** It wraps a small backend and the
large side the deployment already had (one adapter, or the fallback chain of ADR 0009). The
use case, the readiness probe and the shutdown hook do not change, and switching it on is
configuration: `LLM_ROUTING=heuristic` and `LLM_SMALL_MODEL`. `LLM_SMALL_PROVIDER` defaults to
the main provider. That covers the usual case, one hosted provider serving a small and a large
model with one key, and also a second local Ollama model. Off by default.

**A rule-based classifier, asymmetric on purpose.** `assess_complexity` (in
`domain/services/query_complexity.py`) is pure and runs in microseconds. A question goes to
the small model only when none of the following fire:

- comparison: compare, difference, better, cheaper, conviene, "qué es mejor"
- explanation: why, explain, por qué, analiza
- calculation: calculate, in total, cuánto me costaría
- synthesis: summary, step by step, overview, worth it, paso a paso
- several questions joined ("…y cuánto tarda?", "…and who pays?")
- two question marks
- two or more different figures
- more than 40 words

Any one is enough for the large model; there is no score to tune. The rules are matched on
lower-cased, accent-free text in English and Spanish, and each stays linear on hostile input,
like the gateway's. One signal points the other way: with no retrieved passages the only
correct answer is an abstention, and the small model gives that as well as the large one.

**The small model can only fail towards the large one.** The small backend has its own circuit
breaker, with the threshold and reset of ADR 0009. If it is unavailable, rate limited or its
circuit is open, the question is answered by the large side. Routing is meant to save money,
so it must not cost availability. A defect (anything but an unavailable backend) is raised, as
in the fallback. In streaming, the small model can be abandoned only before its first text,
for the same reason as in the fallback.

**No second opinion.** When the small model answers, that answer is final. Asking the large
model as well when the small one abstains or looks unsure would cost more than routing saves,
and would need a quality signal the pipeline does not compute before answering.

**Measured, not asserted.** `scripts/evaluate_router.py` runs the classifier over
`evals/routing/dataset.jsonl`: the 141 retrieval-set questions, labelled with a rubric fixed
before the first run, plus 50 complex and 15 simple questions written for this evaluation. On
the first run:

| | Result |
|---|---|
| Complex questions kept on the large model | 82.7% (43/52) |
| Simple questions moved to the small model | 97.4% (150/154) |
| Retrieval-set questions sent to the small model | 98.6% (139/141) |

The same run found two defects in the rules, which were fixed (`differen` missed the verb
"differ", and "qué es mejor" was missing). The current figures are generated into
[`docs/ROUTER_EVALUATION.md`](../ROUTER_EVALUATION.md), and a test fails if that file is stale.

## Consequences

- With routing on, most real questions are answered by the small model. What that saves
  depends on the price gap between the two models; `aegis_llm_cost_usd_total` is already per
  model. `LLM_SMALL_INPUT_PRICE_PER_MTOK` and `LLM_SMALL_OUTPUT_PRICE_PER_MTOK` give the small
  model its own price, because on one provider both models share a host and pricing used to be
  keyed by host alone.
- **Questions that combine two policies without saying so are the weak spot:** only 1 of 8
  such questions in the dataset stays on the large model. "I'm a Gold member in
  Quetzaltenango. Do I pay for installation?" has none of the signals above; only the
  documents show that it needs two of them. The candidate fix is a signal from the retrieved
  passages (several documents with close scores), which needs measuring against real retrieval
  before it is trusted.
- **This measures the routing decision, not the answers.** Whether the configured small model
  answers what it is sent as well as the large one is a live measurement, with the two models
  and the faithfulness judge (ADR 0017), and it has not been run yet. Until it has, the saving
  is known and the quality cost is an estimate.
- A small model on a hosted provider behind a local large one sends the questions it receives
  off the host. `Settings.hosted_llm_roles` reports it, and startup logs it at warning level
  like any other hosted backend. A local small model behind a hosted large one does the
  opposite: simple questions never leave the host.
- A local small model is a second Ollama model kept resident next to the first, at the cost
  of its memory (see the keep-alive note in ADR 0009).
- New signals: `aegis_llm_routes_total{tier, reason}` (`reason` is the first signal that fired,
  `plain_lookup` or `no_context`), `aegis_llm_route_escalations_total{reason}`, the small
  model's circuit in `aegis_llm_circuit_open`, and `aegis.llm_tier` on the generation span.

## Alternatives considered

- **A small LLM as the classifier.** Better at the combined-policy case, but it adds a model
  call (latency and cost) to every question to save some on most, and a prompt-injectable
  component in front of the decision. Rejected for now.
- **An embedding classifier trained on labelled questions.** Cheap at runtime, since the query
  is already embedded for retrieval. It needs a labelled set far larger than this one to
  generalise, and its decisions cannot be explained per question. Worth revisiting once
  production traffic exists to label.
- **A cascade: the small model first, the large one when the answer looks weak.** It needs a
  quality signal before the answer is returned. The faithfulness judge is that signal but is
  optional and slow on CPU, and every escalated question would pay for both models. Rejected.
- **Routing by the length of the retrieved context.** Long contexts are not harder questions
  here: retrieval returns a fixed number of passages of a fixed size.
- **A gateway product with routing** (LiteLLM, a cloud AI gateway). Same objection as in ADR
  0009: it moves cost accounting and failure handling out of this codebase.
